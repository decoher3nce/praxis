# Praxis

*A leadership-development system for producing more leaders, not more followers.*

> "The function of leadership is to produce more leaders, not more followers."
>
> Ralph Nader

---

## What Praxis is

Praxis is a coaching toolkit for managers who want to grow the judgment of the people they lead, not just their output. It is built for a specific, demanding audience: technically credible engineers in a mission-assurance culture who are allergic to corporate-coaching fluff. It assumes you are smart, busy, and skeptical, and it tries to earn its place on that basis.

**Praxis** means putting theory into practice and refining it through reflection. That is exactly the method this system runs on: you learn to lead by *doing*, reflecting honestly on what happened, and doing it again with sharper judgment. The kit is built to make that loop deliberate, and to ground it in what's actually known about how people learn (deliberate practice, spacing, retrieval, specific feedback) rather than in motivational slogans.

Two ideas anchor it:

- **The method:** action → reflection → better action, run as a deliberate loop on real work.
- **The telos** (Nader, above): the point of leading is to make more leaders.

Everything in this kit serves those ideas. The tone throughout is invitational, evidence-respecting, and aimed at growing the student's own judgment, not at installing yours.

## The seven-step loop

Praxis runs as a repeating engagement. The coach drives steps 1–3; steps 4–7 are collaborative and cycle.

1. **Observe & nominate.** You observe the student and identify *a few* dimensions worth developing. (Or they bring their own; or you start from a recent review. Any entry point is valid.)
2. **Contract (opt-in).** A short, explicit agreement. The student can decline the whole thing. Consent is the foundation, not a formality.
3. **Present a few, student chooses one.** You offer the nominated dimensions *invitationally*. They pick one, or pass this cycle. Selection is gated by readiness (relevance + bandwidth).
4. **Build the path.** For the chosen dimension, assemble a short, multimodal learning path (a reading, a talk, an observation task) plus a sequenced set of practice activities on real work.
5. **Practice.** The student runs the activities against live work, not exercises.
6. **Follow up & adjust.** Recurring check-ins: debrief the activity, then *tweak it, keep it, or swap it.* This is the loop in motion.
7. **Reflect & close.** Pre- and post-engagement, both parties reflect on the dimension and decide whether to continue, switch, or graduate the student toward developing others.

**A suggested first pass:** read `08-intro-lecture.md` for the orientation, then `01-coach-guide.md` end to end, skim a few dimension files in `04-dimensions/` (start with `_index.md`), and read the 2×2 spec in `05-reflection-2x2.md`. That's enough to run a first contracting conversation.

**On the dimensions.** The library is **18 dimensions**, synthesized from a leadership 360 (The Leadership Circle), a wide cross-domain brainstorm, the Gallup engagement research, and the irreducible universal-skills core. They form an interconnected **web, not a ranking** (most skills live under several dimensions at once), so there is no fixed order and no single entry point. The coaching situation picks the doorway. The intro lecture and `04-dimensions/_index.md` explain how to navigate it.

**The 18 are not exhaustive.** They're a strong starting set, not a closed taxonomy. There are gaps, and that's expected. Use your judgment and work on whatever dimension fits the student in front of you, including ones not yet written here. The system is meant to grow: add dimensions, capabilities, and skills as you discover them.

## Everything is a menu, not a mandate

This is the most important usage note. Praxis is flexible by design. The coach and student co-decide at every step. The dimensions are options. The activities are options. The learning resources are fuel, not homework. A student can decline a cycle, pause mid-cycle, or swap a dimension. None of that is failure. It is the system working as intended. A development push delivered at the wrong time is a burden, not a gift.

## Caveats

**This is a dual role.** If you are using Praxis with your direct reports, you are their manager *and* their coach. Coaching conversations are not fully confidential. They can bear on performance and advancement. The expectations handout names this honestly rather than pretending otherwise. Do not promise a confidentiality you cannot keep; false promises here destroy the trust the whole system depends on. See `02-coaching-expectations.md`.

**Templates only. No real people live here.** Every file in this kit is a blank, reusable template. There are no named reports, no per-person records, no coaching logs with real data. Filled-in, per-person instances belong in your organization's approved work systems (your HR/performance tooling, your sanctioned notes system), not in this kit and not in a personal vault. Keep the template clean and keep the data where it's governed.

## On sources

A described gap is better than a confident hallucination.
