# Readiness & Timing

*Gate for step 3. Before a dimension is selected, two conditions should be roughly met. This is a quick check, not a bureaucratic hurdle. But skipping it is the most common way a coaching cycle quietly fails.*

The principle: **a development push at the wrong time is a burden, not a gift.** Timing is not a detail; it is the difference between practice that lands on real work and practice that dies on the vine.

---

## Condition 1: Relevance / recency

The dimension should lightly coincide with something live in the student's current work: a recent event, a current project, or a recent piece of feedback. Recent-past anchoring beats abstract aspiration, because the practice needs somewhere real to land.

**Quick check, ask:**
- "Where in your current or upcoming work would you actually practice this?"
- "Is there a recent moment (a meeting, a decision, a piece of feedback) that this connects to?"
- "If we picked this, what's the real thing it would show up in over the next month?"

**Reading the answer:**
- **Strong relevance:** they name a specific, upcoming, real situation without much prompting. Green light.
- **Weak relevance:** the dimension is aspirational but has no live landing spot ("I'd like to be more strategic someday"). The practice will stall at the visible-stretch step. Either find the live hook together, or choose a different dimension that has one now.

A dimension with no current home is not wrong. It's just early. Park it.

---

## Condition 2: Bandwidth

The student should not be buried by work or family load. This is *the* reason the right to decline matters. Asking someone to take on deliberate development during a launch crunch, an anomaly investigation, or a hard stretch at home is asking them to do worse at the thing that actually matters right now.

**Quick check, ask:**
- "Honestly, do you have room for this right now?"
- "What does your work and life load look like over the next month or so?"
- "On a scale of underwater to coasting, where are you?"

**Reading the answer:**
- **Adequate bandwidth:** they have slack for one deliberate stretch without it crowding out the day job. Green light.
- **Low bandwidth:** they're at or past capacity. The right move is to wait. Say so plainly and set a revisit date.

**Make low-bandwidth honesty safe.** People will hide overload if admitting it feels like weakness. Pre-empt that:
- "If now isn't the time, that's the right answer, not a cop-out."
- "I'd rather you tell me you're slammed than push through and have this become one more thing."

---

## How to honor a decline

A decline, whether of the whole engagement, of a cycle, or of timing, must be genuinely cost-free, or you will never get an honest one again. The integrity of every future "yes" depends on how you handle a "no."

**When a student declines or defers:**

1. **Accept it cleanly.** No visible disappointment, no negotiation, no "are you sure?" three times. One confirmation is respect; three is pressure.
2. **Affirm the judgment.** "Good call. Knowing when not to take something on is exactly the kind of judgment we're trying to grow." Mean it. Declining at the right time *is* leadership.
3. **Leave the door open without leaning on it.** "Revisit whenever: bring it to me, or I'll check back in a month if that's useful." Let them control the re-entry.
4. **Actually let it go.** Do not bring it up sideways, do not let it color a review, do not treat the person as having failed a test. If a decline ever has a cost, you have converted opt-in into coercion and the whole system is dead.

**The test:** if a student can say "not now" to your face and feel completely fine afterward, your Praxis practice is healthy. If they can't, fix that before anything else.

## Quick readiness summary

| | Relevance present | Relevance weak |
|---|---|---|
| **Bandwidth present** | **Go.** Build the path. | Find the live hook, or pick a different dimension. |
| **Bandwidth low** | **Wait.** Set a revisit date. | **Wait.** This is clearly not the cycle. |

## References

- **Adult learning / andragogy** (Malcolm Knowles): adults engage when learning is relevant to a real, current need and is self-directed, which is why the relevance check matters.
