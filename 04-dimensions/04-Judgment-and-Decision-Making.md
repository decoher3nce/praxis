# Judgment & Decision-Making

**Definition.** Making sound calls under uncertainty: matching the rigor and speed of a decision to its stakes and reversibility, reasoning from the available evidence, and owning the outcome.

**Why it matters here.** Mission-assurance work is a chain of judgment calls made with incomplete data and real consequences: ship or retest, accept the anomaly or chase it, waive or hold. A leader with strong judgment doesn't just make good calls; they make their *reasoning* legible, which is how the next generation learns to decide well without being handed the answer. That legibility is the developing-others payoff hidden inside this dimension.

**Behavioral indicators.** Distinguishes reversible from irreversible decisions and spends rigor accordingly; states assumptions and what would change their mind; decides at the right speed (neither freezing on small calls nor rushing big ones); separates the quality of a decision from the luck of its outcome; documents the reasoning, not just the verdict.

**Lower vs. higher.**
- *Lower:* Treats all decisions with the same weight: agonizes over trivial calls, rushes consequential ones. Confuses a good outcome with a good decision. Reasoning lives only in their head. Freezes when the data is incomplete (often a perfectionism tell).
- *Higher:* Calibrates rigor to stakes and reversibility: fast on two-way doors, careful on one-way doors. Names assumptions and the disconfirming evidence they looked for. Decides under uncertainty and owns it. Makes the reasoning visible so others can learn and challenge it.

**Learning path.**
- *Read:* a short piece on reversible vs. irreversible decisions (one-way vs. two-way doors), or on separating decision quality from outcome. `[verify: describe topic if no confident specific source]`
- *Watch:* a talk on decision-making under uncertainty or cognitive bias in expert judgment. `[verify]`
- *Other:* interview a leader you respect about a hard call they got right and one they got wrong, focusing on the reasoning, not the result.

**Activity sequence.**
1. **Frame & baseline.** Self-rate 1–5. Take a current decision and label it: reversible or not? High or low stakes? *(low-stakes)*
2. **Input.** Engage the learning path; adopt one frame (e.g., two-way vs. one-way doors). *(low-stakes)*
3. **Observe.** For a week, before each decision note: stakes, reversibility, and the one assumption you're leaning on. *(low-stakes)*
4. **Small experiment.** On one low-stakes reversible call, deliberately decide *faster* than instinct. On one other, write your reasoning and what would change your mind before deciding. *(low-stakes)*
5. **Visible stretch.** On a real, consequential decision, write a brief decision memo *before* acting (the call, key assumptions, disconfirming evidence you sought, why now) and share it. *(visible stretch)*
6. **Reflect & integrate.** From memory, revisit a past decision: was it a good decision regardless of outcome? Where did luck get confused with skill? Debrief with coach. *(retrieval)*
7. **Re-rate & decide.** Both rate; plot on the 2×2; decide continue / tweak / swap / graduate.

**Coach talking points (this dimension).**
- "Is this a one-way door or a two-way door? Are you spending the right rigor for which one it is?"
- "What's the assumption this whole decision rests on, and what would change your mind?"
- "Was that a bad decision, or a good decision with a bad outcome? How can you tell?"
- "Where are you frozen on a small reversible call, and where are you rushing a big irreversible one?"
- *(Blind-spot version, self-high/coach-low):* "Let's walk through a recent call together: the reasoning at the time, not what you know now."
