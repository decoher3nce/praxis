# Perception & Sense-Making

**Definition.** Seeing what others miss (weak signals, the thing off by a hair, the pattern across cases) and turning ambiguous reality into a workable interpretation the team can act on. *(This is situational awareness plus the act of making meaning from it.)*

**Why it matters here.** Half of test and inspection work *is* perception: the anomaly that doesn't announce itself, the data that's subtly wrong, the assumption nobody stated. And in a VUCA environment, the leader is the team's reality-organizer; when things are ambiguous, people look to someone to say "here's what's going on and what it means." A leader who senses well and frames clearly keeps a team oriented when the ground is moving.

**Behavioral indicators.** Notices the detail or weak signal others walk past; asks "what's actually going on here?" before reacting; frames ambiguous situations so the team knows what to do; thinks in systems (sees second- and third-order effects); leads by asking the sharper question rather than asserting the answer.

**Lower vs. higher.**
- *Lower:* Takes situations at face value; misses the early signal until it's a problem. Reacts to events without framing them. Optimizes the local piece without seeing the whole. Fills ambiguity with the first convenient story.
- *Higher:* Perceives the anomaly early and takes it seriously. Pauses to frame before the team charges off. Reasons about the whole system and downstream effects. Holds ambiguity open long enough to make sense of it, then gives the team a clear read.

**Learning path.**
- *Read:* a short piece on sensemaking (e.g., Weick), situational awareness, or systems thinking. `[verify: describe topic if no confident specific source]`
- *Watch:* a talk on noticing, perception, or thinking in systems. `[verify]`
- *Other:* spend 30 minutes observing a process or meeting with the single job of noticing what's *not* being said or seen.

**Activity sequence.**
1. **Frame & baseline.** Self-rate 1–5. Recall a recent surprise: was there an early signal you now realize you missed? *(low-stakes)*
2. **Input.** Engage the learning path; pick one lens (weak signals, systems, sensemaking). *(low-stakes)*
3. **Observe.** For a week, in each meeting jot the one thing nobody's naming: the unstated assumption, the second-order effect, the quiet signal. *(low-stakes)*
4. **Small experiment.** In one situation, before the team dives into solving, ask "what's actually going on here, and what might we be missing?" *(low-stakes)*
5. **Visible stretch.** Take one genuinely ambiguous situation you own, make sense of it, and give your team a clear, written framing: what's happening, what it means, what we do. *(visible stretch)*
6. **Reflect & integrate.** From memory: where did slowing down to perceive change the read? Where did you still take something at face value? Debrief with coach. *(retrieval)*
7. **Re-rate & decide.** Both rate; plot on the 2×2; decide continue / tweak / swap / graduate.

**Coach talking points (this dimension).**
- "What's the signal you're seeing that nobody's talking about yet?"
- "Before we solve this, what's actually going on here? What are we assuming?"
- "If this plays out, what happens two steps downstream that no one's looking at?"
- "Where did a recent surprise actually start? Was the signal there earlier?"
- *(Blind-spot version, self-high/coach-low):* "You read situations quickly. Can we slow one down together and check what the fast read might have skipped?"
