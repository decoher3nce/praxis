# Caring

**Definition.** Genuinely valuing the people you lead as whole humans (their growth, their wellbeing, their life beyond the work) and caring in a way they actually *feel*.

**Why it matters here.** This is one of the most direct drivers of engagement there is: Gallup's research on what makes teams thrive includes, prominently, having someone at work who genuinely cares about you as a person. Felt care is the foundation of discretionary effort and retention; people give their best to leaders they believe are in their corner. And it's load-bearing for the whole Praxis system: people don't take development risks, admit they're struggling, or stretch in front of you unless they trust you actually care about them, not just their output. You cannot develop someone you don't care about, and they can tell.

**Behavioral indicators.** Knows what matters to each person beyond the work; notices when someone's off and follows up; protects people's wellbeing and bandwidth, even at a cost to short-term output; shows care in the form *that person* receives (not a one-size gesture); remembers and asks about the things people told them.

**Lower vs. higher.**
- *Lower:* Relates to people primarily as resources or roles. Doesn't notice (or notices and doesn't act) when someone's struggling. Care, if present, stays internal and unfelt. Treats "caring" as a uniform script rather than knowing the individual.
- *Higher:* Knows their people as individuals with lives. Catches the off-day and checks in. Will spend something real to protect a person's wellbeing. Tailors care to what each person actually values, which requires knowing them.

**Learning path.**
- *Read:* Gallup, Q12 Item 5 ("My supervisor, or someone at work, seems to care about me as a person"): the engagement research linking felt care to safety, retention, and performance. (See Gallup's Q12 materials.)
- *Watch:* Simon Sinek, "Why good leaders make you feel safe" (TED 2014): leadership as taking care of the people in your charge, and the "circle of safety."
- *Other:* in your next few 1:1s, spend the first ten minutes on the person, not the work, and just listen.

**Activity sequence.**
1. **Frame & baseline.** Self-rate 1–5. For each person you lead, write one thing that matters to them outside work. Notice the gaps. *(low-stakes)*
2. **Input.** Engage the learning path. *(low-stakes)*
3. **Observe.** For a week, notice signs of how each person is actually doing (energy, mood, load) without acting yet. *(low-stakes)*
4. **Small experiment.** In one 1:1, lead with the person: ask how they're really doing and follow the thread, instead of jumping to status. *(low-stakes)*
5. **Visible stretch.** Act on care in a way that costs you something real (protect someone's bandwidth, adjust a deadline for a person who's underwater, or advocate for them) and do it in the form *they'd* value. *(visible stretch)*
6. **Reflect & integrate.** From memory: what did you learn about your people that you didn't know? Where had care stayed internal and unfelt? Debrief with coach. *(retrieval)*
7. **Re-rate & decide.** Both rate; plot on the 2×2; decide continue / tweak / swap / graduate.

**Coach talking points (this dimension).**
- "What matters to each of your people *outside* the work? Where are the blanks?"
- "Who on your team is struggling right now, and how would you know if you were wrong?"
- "When did you last spend something real to protect a person's wellbeing?"
- "Does your care actually land for *them*, in a form they value, or in the form that's easy for you?"
- *(Blind-spot version, self-high/coach-low):* "You feel you show up for your people. Can we check that against how cared-for they'd say they feel? The gap there is worth looking at gently."

**Connected dimensions.** Close neighbor of **Trust & Safety (08)** (care is the human-regard half, safety the reliability half) and built on the listening/empathy skills in **Perception & Sense-Making (05)**. It's the relational soil **Developing Others (10)** grows in, and it draws steadiness from **Presence (12)**.
