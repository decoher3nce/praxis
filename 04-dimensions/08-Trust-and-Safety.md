# Trust & Safety

**Definition.** Being someone people will follow and tell the truth to (through reliability and consistency) and creating the conditions where people raise problems early, dissent safely, and admit mistakes fast.

**Why it matters here.** This is arguably the most mission-critical relational dimension in a test organization, and the most underrated. Nearly every catastrophic aerospace failure has a chapter where someone knew and didn't say, or said and wasn't heard. Psychological safety is the mechanism that gets bad news *up* the chain in time to act. Trust is also the soil the whole Praxis system grows in: people don't develop, take risks, or admit they need to learn unless it's safe to be imperfect in front of you.

**Behavioral indicators.** Reliable and consistent: does what they said, the same way whether or not it's convenient; responds to bad news with curiosity, not blame; admits their own mistakes openly; actively invites dissent and thanks the person who raises the hard thing; reads people's states and motives accurately; separates the problem from the person in a failure review.

**Lower vs. higher.**
- *Lower:* Inconsistent: people can't predict which version of them they'll get. Reacts to bad news in a way that teaches people to stop bringing it. Looks for who to blame. Rarely admits error. Dissent quietly dries up; agreeable silence gets mistaken for alignment.
- *Higher:* Consistent and reliable, so trust compounds. Treats an early-surfaced problem as a gift and says so. Models fallibility by owning their own mistakes first. Explicitly invites the dissenting view. Runs blameless reviews focused on the system, not the scapegoat.

**Learning path.**
- *Read:* Charles Duhigg, "What Google Learned From Its Quest to Build the Perfect Team," *The New York Times Magazine* (February 2016): Project Aristotle's finding that psychological safety mattered more than who was on the team.
- *Watch:* Amy Edmondson, "Building a psychologically safe workplace" (TEDxHGSE, 2014): what psychological safety is, why people stay silent, and why it has to pair with accountability.
- *Other:* ask your team (anonymously if needed) "what's something you've hesitated to tell me?" and respond in a way that proves it was safe.

**Activity sequence.**
1. **Frame & baseline.** Self-rate 1–5. Ask honestly: when did someone last bring you bad news early, and how did you react? *(low-stakes)*
2. **Input.** Engage the learning path; pick one safety behavior to practice. *(low-stakes)*
3. **Observe.** For a week, watch your own reactions to problems and disagreement. Does your face and tone invite more, or shut it down? *(low-stakes)*
4. **Small experiment.** In one meeting, explicitly invite the dissenting view ("what am I missing? who sees this differently?") and visibly thank whoever speaks up. *(low-stakes)*
5. **Visible stretch.** Run one failure or anomaly review as genuinely blameless (whole conversation on the system and the learning, not who), or admit one of your own mistakes openly to the team. *(visible stretch)*
6. **Reflect & integrate.** From memory: did people raise more or less after your responses this cycle? Where did you accidentally punish candor? Debrief with coach. *(retrieval)*
7. **Re-rate & decide.** Both rate; plot on the 2×2; decide continue / tweak / swap / graduate.

**Coach talking points (this dimension).**
- "When something goes wrong, what does your team see on your face first, curiosity or blame?"
- "When did someone last tell you something you didn't want to hear? What happened to them after?"
- "If your team's gone quiet and agreeable, is that alignment, or silence?"
- "What mistake of your own could you put on the table to make it safe for others?"
- *(Blind-spot version, self-high/coach-low):* "You feel the team's open with you. Can we find a low-risk way to check whether they'd agree? I want to test my read against theirs."

**Connected dimensions.** Built on **Courage & Integrity (03)** (consistency, walking the talk) and **Caring (07)** (its human-regard twin). Its inviting-dissent skill is shared with **Conflict & Collaboration (11)**; reading people draws on **Perception & Sense-Making (05)**; and it's the precondition that makes **Developing Others (10)** possible at all.
