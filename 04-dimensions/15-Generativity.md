# Generativity

**Definition.** Originating genuinely new work, and, just as important, creating the conditions in which others generate rather than merely execute.

**Why it matters here.** Mission assurance is mostly disciplined repetition, but the hard problems (a novel failure mode, a test no one has run, a method that doesn't exist yet) demand origination. A leader who is generative expands what the team can attempt; one who only optimizes the known eventually gets stuck when the known runs out. And the multiplier version matters most: a leader who makes it safe and rewarding for *others* to generate builds an inventive team, not an audience for their own ideas.

**Behavioral indicators.** Generates novel options instead of only choosing among given ones; protects fragile early ideas from premature judgment; recombines existing things into something new; builds conditions (time, safety, divergence) for others' creativity; distinguishes the divergent phase (generate freely) from the convergent phase (decide).

**Lower vs. higher.**
- *Lower:* Works only within the existing menu of options; defaults to "how it's always been done." Kills nascent ideas with early criticism (theirs or others'). Treats creativity as someone else's job. Converges too fast, before real options exist.
- *Higher:* Produces original options and frames new problems. Shields early ideas long enough to develop. Actively cultivates others' generativity. Knows when to open up wide and when to narrow down.

**Learning path.**
- *Read:* a short piece on creativity, idea generation, or separating divergent from convergent thinking. `[verify: describe topic if no confident specific source]`
- *Watch:* a talk on creativity, innovation, or how new ideas actually form. `[verify]`
- *Other:* talk to someone known for inventive work about how they generate and protect early ideas.

**Activity sequence.**
1. **Frame & baseline.** Self-rate 1–5. Take a current problem and force yourself to generate five options, not one. *(low-stakes)*
2. **Input.** Engage the learning path; note the divergent/convergent distinction. *(low-stakes)*
3. **Observe.** For a week, notice when you (or the team) converge too fast, or kill an early idea before it could grow. *(low-stakes)*
4. **Small experiment.** Run one short divergent session (generate many options with no judgment allowed) *before* deciding anything. *(low-stakes)*
5. **Visible stretch.** Tackle one real problem generatively: create the conditions for the team to produce novel options, protect the fragile ones, and only then converge. *(visible stretch)*
6. **Reflect & integrate.** From memory: where did holding off judgment produce a better option? Where did you or others reach for the default? Debrief with coach. *(retrieval)*
7. **Re-rate & decide.** Both rate; plot on the 2×2; decide continue / tweak / swap / graduate.

**Coach talking points (this dimension).**
- "Is this the only option, or the first one? What are three others?"
- "Where did a good idea get killed too early, by the team, or by you?"
- "Are you in 'generate' mode or 'decide' mode right now? Are you sure it's the right one?"
- "How do you make it safe and worthwhile for your people to bring half-formed ideas?"
- *(Blind-spot version, self-high/coach-low):* "You value creativity. Can we look at whether the team actually experiences room to generate, or mostly executes your ideas?"
