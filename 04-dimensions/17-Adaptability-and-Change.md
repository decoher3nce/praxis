# Adaptability & Change

**Definition.** Adjusting your own course as conditions shift, and bringing people through change in a way that keeps them oriented and engaged, rather than anxious or resistant.

**Why it matters here.** This is the dimension VUCA most directly demands. Programs re-baseline, requirements churn, anomalies rewrite the plan, reorgs happen. A leader who only executes the original plan becomes brittle; one who adapts well keeps the mission on track through turbulence. Leading change well is also a multiplier: people brought through change with clarity and agency learn to lead others through it later, instead of just enduring it.

**Behavioral indicators.** Updates the plan when the facts change, without ego about the original; communicates the *why* behind a change, not just the *what*; gives people agency within change rather than imposing it whole; names and manages the human side of change (uncertainty, loss); responds to what actually emerges in real time ("yes-and") rather than forcing the planned reality.

**Lower vs. higher.**
- *Lower:* Clings to the original plan past its expiry; treats change as a threat. Announces changes as faits accomplis, "why" omitted. Leaves people to absorb the uncertainty alone. Either rigid (won't bend) or chaotic (bends with every wind).
- *Higher:* Reads the shift early and re-plans without defensiveness. Brings people the reasoning and a real role in shaping the response. Acknowledges the human cost openly. Stays a stable reference point while the work flexes: adaptable, not unmoored.

**Learning path.**
- *Read:* a short piece on leading through change, managing the *transition* (not just the change), or strong-opinions-loosely-held. `[verify: describe topic if no confident specific source]`
- *Watch:* a talk on adaptability, change leadership, or navigating uncertainty. `[verify]`
- *Other:* talk to someone who led a team through a hard change about what they'd do differently.

**Activity sequence.**
1. **Frame & baseline.** Self-rate 1–5. Name a current or recent change your team faced, and honestly assess how well you brought them through it. *(low-stakes)*
2. **Input.** Engage the learning path; pick one lens (communicate the why, give agency, manage the transition). *(low-stakes)*
3. **Observe.** For a week, notice your own reaction when plans shift: do you flex or dig in? And watch how your team takes its cue from you. *(low-stakes)*
4. **Small experiment.** On one small change, communicate the *why* before the *what*, and ask the team for input on the *how*. *(low-stakes)*
5. **Visible stretch.** Lead the team through one real, consequential change: re-plan openly, explain the reasoning, give people genuine agency, and name the hard parts honestly. *(visible stretch)*
6. **Reflect & integrate.** From memory: where did you adapt vs. cling, and how did the team respond to how you handled it? Debrief with coach. *(retrieval)*
7. **Re-rate & decide.** Both rate; plot on the 2×2; decide continue / tweak / swap / graduate.

**Coach talking points (this dimension).**
- "The facts changed. Is the plan still right, or are you holding it because it's yours?"
- "When you rolled out that change, did people get the *why*, or just the *what*?"
- "Where in this change can people have real agency, instead of just absorbing it?"
- "What's the human cost of this change that nobody's naming, and what happens if you name it?"
- *(Blind-spot version, self-high/coach-low):* "You feel you led that change well. Can we ask the team how the transition actually felt from where they sat?"
