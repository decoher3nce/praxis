# The 2×2 Reflection Instrument

*The pre/post measurement at the heart of a Praxis cycle. Both parties rate the chosen dimension (once before the work, once after) and compare. The point is not the score. The point is the **gap** between how the student sees themselves and how the coach sees them, and how that gap **moves**.*

---

## The two axes

- **Self-assessment:** the student rates their own current capability on the dimension, low → high.
- **Coach assessment:** the coach rates the student's current capability on the dimension, low → high.

Use whatever scale you like (1–5 works well). The exact number matters far less than which quadrant the pairing lands in.

## The matrix

| | **Coach: Low** | **Coach: High** |
|---|---|---|
| **Self: High** | **Blind spot** *(overestimation)* | **Confirmed strength** *(self-aware high performer)* |
| **Self: Low** | **Shared growth area** *(acknowledged gap)* | **Hidden strength** *(underestimation; often impostor-flavored)* |

### Blind spot: Self high, Coach low (overestimation)

The most delicate conversation in the whole system. The student believes they're strong here; you don't see it yet. Handled badly, this triggers defensiveness and ends the learning. Handled well, it's where the biggest growth lives.

- **Lead with specific evidence and curiosity, never verdicts.** "Can we look at a couple of recent examples together? I want to check my read against yours."
- Put behavior on the table, not judgment about the person.
- Your goal in the first conversation is not agreement on the score. It's *awareness that a gap might exist.* That alone is a win.

### Confirmed strength: Self high, Coach high (self-aware high performer)

Aligned at the top. They're strong and they know it, accurately. **This is where the Nader loop closes:** the highest-value move is to have them coach *someone else* in this dimension. Leverage the strength outward; don't just admire it. (See graduation in `01-coach-guide.md`.)

### Shared growth area: Self low, Coach low (acknowledged gap)

The easiest alignment: you both already agree there's room to grow. No painful gap to surface, so trust is cheap and momentum is fast. **Often the best place to start a coaching cycle,** especially a first one: it builds the working relationship before you ever have to navigate a blind spot.

### Hidden strength: Self low, Coach high (underestimation)

The student sells themselves short; you see more capability than they credit. Often impostor-flavored. **The work here is ownership and confidence, not remediation.** Don't "fix" a strength. Help them claim it.

- "You rated yourself low here. Let me tell you what I actually saw." Then give specific evidence of the strength.
- Build the habit of owning it: where could they step into this more visibly?

---

## How to run the pre-rating

1. **Have the student rate first, privately and honestly.** Make clear there's no right answer and no reward for modesty or bravado. "I want your real read, not the flattering one or the humble one."
2. **You rate independently**, grounded in specific observed behavior. Use the dimension's behavioral indicators so you're rating actions, not vibes.
3. **Compare and locate the quadrant together.** Name it without loading it: "Looks like we're both seeing room here" (shared growth) or "Interesting, I'm seeing more than you are" (hidden strength).

## How to surface the gap respectfully

The general rule across every quadrant: **describe what you observed; let them interpret.** Specific behavior is hard to argue with and easy to discuss; a verdict invites defense.

- Blind spot: maximum care. Evidence + curiosity, aim for awareness not agreement.
- Hidden strength: evidence + encouragement, aim for ownership.
- Shared growth: name it plainly and get to work.
- Confirmed strength: name it, then point it outward toward coaching others.

## What movement means (the real measure)

Re-rate post-cycle and read the *movement between pre and post*. That's the signal, more than the absolute score:

- **Blind spot → Shared growth** = **awareness gained.** The student now sees the gap you saw. This is a real win *even before the skill improves*. You cannot develop what you can't see. Celebrate it as progress, not as a downgrade.
- **Shared growth → Confirmed (or near it)** = **skill built.** The straightforward success path: agreed gap, closed gap.
- **Hidden strength → Confirmed strength** = **confidence claimed.** The skill was always there; now they own it. Watch for the readiness to coach others.
- **Stayed put** = information, not failure. Maybe the activity was wrong (tweak/swap), the timing was off (pause), or more reps are needed (keep). Use the check-in decision rule.

**Frame the whole instrument around movement and self-awareness, not a high number.** A student who moves from blind spot to shared growth has done harder and more valuable work than one who nudged a 4 to a 5. Score the journey, not the altitude.

## References

- **Johari Window** (Joseph Luft and Harrington Ingham, 1955): the self-versus-other awareness quadrants this 2×2 adapts for coaching.
- **360-degree feedback:** the blind-spot and hidden-strength dynamics come from the gap between how a person rates themselves and how others rate them.
