# An Introduction to Leadership Dimensions

*A starting lecture for coaches and students entering the Praxis system. Read it once before your first session. It is written for both roles. Where the two diverge, it says so.*

---

## Why we're doing this at all

There's a line from Ralph Nader that the whole system is built on:

> "The function of leadership is to produce more leaders, not more followers."

That is the entire point. Not to make you a better follower of me, or a better executor of someone else's plan, but to grow your own judgment to the point where you can do hard things no one told you how to do, and then grow that capacity in someone else. A leader is *produced*, not installed. You can't be lectured into one. You become one by acting, reflecting on what happened, and acting again with sharper judgment.

That last sentence is the method, and it is what gives the system its name. **Praxis** means putting ideas into use: you take a skill and practice it on real work until it becomes a natural habit, and reflection is what turns the doing into judgment. Nothing here gets poured into you from the outside. You'll read a little, try things on real work, reflect on how it went, and adjust. The reflection is where the learning actually lives, and it is grounded in what's known about how people learn (deliberate practice, spacing, retrieval), not in slogans.

So if you're a **student**: this is not a performance correction and not a course you pass. It's an invitation to develop, on your terms, at your pace, with the right to decline.

If you're a **coach**: your job is not to transfer your judgment into your student. It's to grow *theirs*. The difference shows up in how much you talk. Ask more than you tell.

## What a "dimension" is

We've organized leadership into **18 dimensions**: territories of capability you can develop. Each one has a file (in `04-dimensions/`) with a definition, the behaviors that show it, what lower and higher capability look like, a light learning path, a sequence of practice activities, and coaching prompts.

Underneath each dimension are **capabilities** (coherent sub-areas) and **skills** (the atomic, practiceable behaviors you actually rep). So the hierarchy is just three layers deep: *dimension → capability → skill.* That's all the structure there is.

Here are the 18, in a rough reading order, but **do not read this as a ranking.** They are not in priority order. Nothing here outranks anything else.

**On yourself:** Self-Awareness · Composure & Regulation · Courage & Integrity
**On thinking:** Judgment & Decision-Making · Perception & Sense-Making · Taste & Discernment
**On people:** Caring · Trust & Safety · Communication & Influence · Developing Others · Conflict & Collaboration · Presence · Play & Levity
**On the work:** Direction & Strategy · Generativity · Execution & Results · Adaptability & Change · Custodianship & Legacy

You'll notice those four groupings (self / thinking / people / work) are just a convenience for finding things. They are not levels. Which brings us to the most important idea in this lecture.

## Leadership is a web, not a ladder

Here is the truth that took us several tries to admit: **these dimensions don't form a hierarchy. They form a web.**

Every one of them is wired to the others through shared skills. *Listening* lives under Perception, under Trust, under Caring, under Communication, and under Developing Others, all at once. So the moment you get better at listening, you've tugged on five dimensions, not one. One dimension is genuinely another's sub-skill, and which is the "parent" depends entirely on which way you're walking the web that day.

We tried to rank them. We tried to nest them in a clean tree. Both collapsed, every time, and that collapse is *information*. It's telling us that leadership doesn't decompose into a tidy outline, because the work itself doesn't.

Which is the right moment to name the acronym you'll hear a lot: **VUCA**: Volatile, Uncertain, Complex, Ambiguous. It's a description of the conditions real leadership happens in. Plans change under you (volatile). You decide without enough information (uncertain). Causes and effects tangle (complex). The same facts support different readings (ambiguous). A test campaign, an anomaly investigation, a re-baselined program: these are VUCA in their bones.

The honest response to VUCA is *not* to pretend you can master it with a better checklist. It's to build the **capacities** that let you operate well inside it: judgment under uncertainty, composure when it's volatile, sense-making when it's ambiguous, adaptability when it's all changing at once. The web of dimensions is VUCA-shaped on purpose, because the work it prepares you for is. Embracing that (being comfortable that it *won't* resolve into a neat ladder) is itself a leadership skill. Maybe the first one.

**For the student, this is freeing:** you don't have to master dimension 1 before you're "allowed" to work on dimension 9. There's no gate, no sequence. You start wherever the web is most alive for you right now.

**For the coach, this is operational:** explore the web *wide* in conversation (follow the threads wherever they lead) but commit *narrow* in practice. Diagnosis ranges across the whole constellation; the actual practice must land on **one** skill at a time. Otherwise "everything's connected" becomes a fog you both hide in. Explore the constellation; practice one star. And here's the payoff: because the web is wired, improving that one skill quietly radiates out to its neighbors. The interconnection that made diagnosis messy makes development efficient.

## Lifelong learning is the core competency

If you forced me to name the one capability that matters most, it wouldn't be on the list of 18. It's the thing the whole list sits inside: **the disposition to keep learning.**

In a stable world you could learn leadership once and coast. In a VUCA world the ground keeps moving, so the only durable advantage is the rate at which you learn and adjust, faster than the problem gets harder. That's not a dimension you check off. It's the *medium* the dimensions live in. It's the praxis loop itself: act, reflect, act again.

This is why "learning" is also the most welcoming place to begin a coaching relationship. Opening with "where do you want to grow?" carries no accusation; it doesn't imply a deficiency, just a direction. It's the lowest-threat door in the building. We'll come back to doors in a second.

**Student:** the bar here is not "be impressive." It's "stay in motion." Half-formed and honestly reflected-on beats polished and faked, every time.

**Coach:** model it. The fastest way to make learning safe is to be visibly learning yourself, including from your student.

## Different people enter through different doors

So if there's no ladder and no required sequence, where does a session actually *start*?

Through a **doorway**, and there are many. A doorway is just a dimension the conversation can naturally open on, from which you can walk to all the others. Some dimensions are especially good doorways because so many threads pass through them:

- **Self-Awareness:** the door into the whole self-cluster. You can't work on composure, presence, or caring without some of this first.
- **Situational Awareness** (we call it *Perception & Sense-Making*): the door into judgment, strategy, and adaptability. The right door for someone strong on self but missing the context around them.
- **Integrity & Values** (*Courage & Integrity*): the foundation trust and credibility are built on. You can't form trust without it, so for some people this is where everything starts.
- **Communication:** touches influence, conflict, developing others, caring. A natural door for anyone whose ideas are right but aren't landing.
- **Learning itself:** the always-open door, because growth is the premise rather than an accusation.

**The crucial point: there is no single correct entry point.** Anyone who tells you "always start with self-awareness" is selling a method, not coaching a person. The *situation* picks the door. A coach reads three things and lets them choose:

1. **Where's the heat?** A recent event, a live project, a piece of review feedback: recent and concrete beats abstract and aspirational. Practice needs somewhere real to land.
2. **What does the student bring?** If they walk in with a concern, that's a door already open. Use it.
3. **What's accessible given trust?** If trust is still thin, don't open on a hard truth. Open on *learning* or on a growth area you both already agree exists. Earn the harder doors.

Different people, different doors, different days. That's not sloppiness; it's the system working as designed.

## How the engagement actually runs

The mechanics are light. The loop:

1. **Observe & nominate:** the coach offers a few dimensions worth developing (or the student brings their own, or you start from a review).
2. **Contract:** a short, explicit, opt-in agreement. (More on this below.)
3. **Present a few, student chooses one:** invitationally. "I'll pass this cycle" is always a real answer.
4. **Build the path:** for the chosen dimension, a short learning path plus a sequence of practice activities on real work.
5. **Practice:** on live work, not exercises.
6. **Check in & adjust:** debrief, then keep / tweak / swap the activity.
7. **Reflect & close:** pre- and post-engagement, you both reflect on the dimension and decide what's next. The goal is movement and self-awareness, not a high score.

Two things every participant should hear out loud at the start:

**This is genuinely opt-in.** You can decline the whole thing, pass on any cycle, or pause when life gets heavy. Declining is not failure; knowing when the timing's wrong is itself the judgment we're trying to grow. A development push at the wrong moment is a burden, not a gift.

**Be straight about the dual role.** In our setting the coach is often also the manager. Pretending coaching is a sealed confidential vault would be a lie, and a lie here poisons the trust the whole thing runs on. Most of what's discussed stays in the coaching space; some of it may bear on the record; and when that line is crossed, the coach will tell you to your face, in the moment. The deal is candor, not secrecy. (The expectations handout is `02-coaching-expectations.md`.)

## Where to go from here

- **Students:** skim a few dimension files in `04-dimensions/`, especially any that connect to something live in your work right now. Come to the first session with a rough sense of where you'd want to grow, or come with nothing and we'll find the door together.
- **Coaches:** read the coach guide (`01-coach-guide.md`) for the talking points by phase, and the readiness check (`03-readiness-and-timing.md`) before you pick a door.

One last time, because it's the whole thing: we are not here to make you a better follower. We are here to produce another leader, and eventually, to have you produce the next one. Welcome.

## References

- **Ralph Nader:** the "produce more leaders, not more followers" line that anchors the system (widely attributed to Nader).
- **VUCA:** Volatile, Uncertain, Complex, Ambiguous; a framing that originated at the U.S. Army War College in the late 1980s (drawing on Bennis and Nanus) and was later widely adopted in strategy and leadership.
- **The science of learning:** deliberate practice, spaced practice, and retrieval practice; fuller citations in `01-coach-guide.md` (Ericsson; *Make It Stick*).
- **Praxis:** the idea of action refined by reflection is an old one in philosophy and education; this system uses its plain meaning (putting ideas into practice), not any single author's version.
